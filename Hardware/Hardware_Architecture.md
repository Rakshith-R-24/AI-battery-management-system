The hardware architecture of the proposed Battery Management System is designed for continuous monitoring and protection of a 4S lithium-ion battery pack. The architecture integrates the battery pack, per-cell voltage sensing, pack current sensing, temperature sensing, an embedded controller, protection circuitry, and monitoring interfaces.

The prototype uses four 18650 lithium-ion cells connected in series, providing a nominal pack voltage of 14.8 V and a maximum voltage of 16.8 V. Since the cells are connected in series, individual cell voltage monitoring is implemented to observe cell-level voltage variation and imbalance during battery operation. Individual cell voltages are measured using voltage-divider sensing circuits and provided to the controller through the available analog measurement interface. The battery current is monitored using an INA226 current-sensing interface, while NTC thermistors are used to monitor the temperature of the battery cells. These sensing stages provide the electrical and thermal parameters required for continuous battery monitoring.

The measured voltage, current, and temperature parameters are acquired by the embedded controller for real-time monitoring and analysis. The controller evaluates the battery operating conditions and provides control signals to the protection stage when abnormal conditions are detected. The protection stage is designed to respond to important battery safety conditions, including overvoltage, undervoltage, overcurrent, short circuit, and overtemperature. Local monitoring and indication interfaces provide information about the battery operating condition and protection status.

For the present hardware validation, the prototype is implemented and tested using an ESP32-based embedded platform. The proposed system architecture is based on the STM32L476RG as the intended embedded controller platform. The separation between the validated prototype platform and the intended controller architecture allows the sensing, monitoring, and protection functions to be developed and evaluated while maintaining a scalable architecture for future higher-series electric-vehicle battery systems.
